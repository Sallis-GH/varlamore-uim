## 2. Sentence and line shape

- **Length:** Lines skew short. Most NPC boxes are one sentence of roughly 5-20 words;
  a second sentence is common but a third is rare. Longer lore/exposition dumps (Metzli,
  Queen Zyanyi Arkan, Curator Haig Halen) stretch to 25-30 words but still break into two or
  three shorter sentences rather than one long clause.
- **Sentences per box:** Typically 1-2. Comedic beats often land as a single short sentence or
  fragment ("Ha! Good one." — *Perilous Moons*; "Nice try." — *The Final Dawn*).
- **Ellipses:** Used constantly for hesitation, trailing off, and dramatic pauses — "This seems
  like a bad idea..." (*The Heart of Darkness*, Prince Itzla Arkan); "I don't remember... It
  seems likely that I would've though." (*Death on the Isle*, Pavo). Rarely more than one
  ellipsis per line.
- **"Er...", "Well...", "Hmm...":** Common hedge-openers before a deflection or joke — "Oh...
  Er... Just delivering some supplies to the guards." (*Children of the Sun*, Guard).
- **Exclamation marks:** Frequent but controlled — used for genuine surprise, comic emphasis,
  or NPC catchphrases ("Kuani!", "Tetamo!", "Nilsal!"), not for constant shouting. Multiple
  NPCs in the same quest can share an exclamation-heavy tic without it reading as spam.
- **British spelling throughout:** "honour", "colour", "favour", "realise", "civilised",
  "defences", "centre", "artefact". Never "honor/color/realize/artifact".
- **Register markers:** Formal NPCs (Queen, Curator, Highlord) use complete, unclipped
  sentences; casual/comic NPCs clip words and use contractions freely ("I'm", "you're",
  "don't").

## 3. Humour catalogue

**Deadpan literalism** — an NPC answers a rhetorical or absurd question completely straight.
- "What's a horse?" → King: "I have no idea." (*Scrambled!*)
- "You're asking me how you should interrogate me?" (*Children of the Sun*, Guard)
- Rule: when the player asks something silly or leading, have the NPC answer the literal
  words, not the intent — the mismatch is the joke.

**Bathos / anticlimax** — a serious setup deflates into something mundane.
- "Ah, excellent! Such should be the fate of all traitors." → "My deepest sympathies." (*Twilight's Promise*, Itzla Arkan)
- "I felt that one from here!" (*Death on the Isle*, fight spectator)
- Rule: follow a dramatic declaration with a small, practical, or petty remark instead of
  more drama.

**Bureaucratic absurdity** — rules-for-rules'-sake and officious pedantry.
- "Well rules are rules, I'm afraid. Without proof, I can't take any orders from you." (*Twilight's Promise*, Knight)
- "Report? I don't think so! It's our day off!" (*Twilight's Promise*, Knight)
- "Not without a uniform you're not." (*Death on the Isle*, Head Butler)
- Rule: give minor officials a rule they enforce over-literally, regardless of the stakes.

**Self-aware / lampshading humour** — a character comments on how absurd their own genre
moment is.
- "A loose cannon? A wild card maybe. Or a lone ranger. But a loose cannon?!" (*Ethically Acquired Antiquities*, Haig Halen)
- "It's always the butler, isn't it." (*Death on the Isle*, Stradius)
- "Only humans would ever manage to reach this level of silliness." (*The Final Dawn*, Attala)
- Rule: let one character name the trope out loud instead of playing it earnestly.

**Dry/gallows wit under danger** — understated commentary while things are going wrong.
- "I am confident that you will probably survive this mission." (*The Heart of Darkness*, Itzla)
- "That thing looks brutal! We don't want to kill him!" (*The Final Dawn*, Captain Vibia)
- "This seems like a bad idea..." (*The Heart of Darkness*, Itzla)
- Rule: understate the danger with a hedge word ("probably", "should be fine") rather than
  panic.

**Running gag / repetition with variation** — a joke that recurs with small twists across a
quest.
- Rotating farmer-couple names in *Scrambled!*: "Rumpty Bumpty," "Gumty Fumty," "Humphrey
  Dumphrey."
- Repeated "Kuani!"/"Tetamo!"/"Nilsal!" greetings across multiple Varlamore quests as a shared
  in-world idiom, not a one-off.
- Rule: pick a small verbal tic (a greeting, a mispronunciation, a name) and reuse it with
  slight variation rather than a single joke.

**Blunt insult / dismissiveness toward the player** — NPCs treating the player as an
unimpressive outsider.
- "A human? Either buy something or get out!" (*Perilous Moons*, Blacksmith)
- "You can admire them from back here!" (*Perilous Moons*, Attala)
- "Sorry, but I'm busy with a different arrogant human. You'll have to wait in line." (*Perilous Moons*, Attala)
- Rule: let non-human or high-status NPCs use "human" as a mild slur/dismissal, not a neutral
  noun.

**Vanity / self-importance** — NPCs who overstate their own competence or status for comic
effect.
- "Blacksmith! Yes, I'm the blacksmith!" (*Scrambled!*)
- "I run something of a shipping company. If you need it moving, Xocotla's Got Ya!" (*Death on the Isle*, Xocotla)
- Rule: have minor NPCs announce their own job title or slogan with unearned pride.

## 4. Refusal and deflection patterns

- **Flat "no" plus a reason clause:** "Sorry, entry here is for guests only." (*Death on the
  Isle*); "I already told you to keep your voice down!" (*The Heart of Darkness*).
- **Redirect to another NPC/object:** "You'll have to go back to Fox and grab it then." (*At
  First Light*); "Then you'd better go and find them." (*Perilous Moons*).
- **Feigned ignorance / stonewalling:** "I don't know what you're talking about!" (*Ethically
  Acquired Antiquities*, Haig Halen); "I'm not telling you anything." (*Children of the Sun*,
  Bandit).
- **Conditional refusal (gate behind a task or item):** "Well, I'd be able to fix more if you
  had more." (*Ethically Acquired Antiquities*, Artima); "The cart must be sorted before I can
  do anything else." (*Scrambled!*, Kauayotl).
- **Refusal wrapped in politeness ("Sorry, but..."):** appears across nearly every quest as
  the default soft-no opener — "Sorry, but you need to be a better hunter before we can help
  you out." (*At First Light*).
- **Comic non-answer / joke deflection:** "If I told you... I'd have to kill you!" (*At First
  Light*, Wolf).
- Rule of thumb: a refusal almost always pairs a short "no" word (Sorry/Well/No/Not) with one
  clause of justification — bare refusals without a reason are rare.

## 5. How NPCs address and treat the player

- **Generic terms of address:** "adventurer" (Regulus Cento), "citizen" (Sergeant Tobyn),
  "human" (dwarves, used dismissively), "iknami" (a local Varlamore greeting/honorific applied
  to the player by Fortis-culture NPCs, e.g. Jatziri, Curator Herminius).
- **Status changes tone sharply:** Guards and low officials are curt and procedural to an
  unknown player ("Move along, citizen."), but warm once the player is vouched for or shows
  authority — "This crest will mark you as a representative of the Queen herself." (*Twilight's
  Promise*, Furia Tullus) triggers immediate deference from knights.
- **Royalty/nobility treat the player as a useful but expendable tool**, mixing genuine respect
  with casual risk-taking: "I am confident that you will probably survive this mission." (*The
  Heart of Darkness*, Itzla Arkan); "I grant you permission to search for these cultists." (*The
  Final Dawn*, Highlord Katlo).
- **Non-human/dwarven NPCs treat the player as an outsider by default**, softening only after
  a task is completed — dwarves in *Perilous Moons* open hostile ("A human? Either buy
  something or get out!") and warm slightly once helpful.
- **Friends/quest allies use first names and informal register** quickly (Fox, Wolf, Kiko in
  *At First Light*), contrasted with formal officials who use full names and titles (Prince
  Itzla Arkan, Queen Zyanyi Arkan, Highlord Katlo).

## 6. Player-line conventions

- The player is allowed **naive/expository questions** ("What can you tell me about
  Varlamore?", "What's a horse?" implicitly triggering deadpan answers) without being mocked
  for asking.
- The player can be **mildly sarcastic or self-deprecating**, but rarely cruel: "Well there's
  nothing like a little dunk to sober you up a bit." (*Twilight's Promise*).
- The player sometimes **narrates their own hesitation or second-guessing** in bracketed
  internal-thought style: "...On second thoughts, I don't like your attitude." (*Perilous
  Moons*).
- Player lines skew slightly **more polite and hedged than NPC lines** ("I'm sorry, but I need
  to head off.", "I probably shouldn't take more than I need."), reinforcing an
  adventurer-as-guest posture in someone else's territory.
- The player readily volunteers competence/backstory when needed for plot ("Well I'm actually
  something of an adventurer myself.") without excessive bragging.

## 7. Option-menu conventions

- **Count:** Typically 2-4 options per menu (occasionally binary Yes/No), rarely more than
  4-5 in these transcripts.
- **Form:** A roughly even mix of questions ("What's going on here?", "Could you tell me more
  about the Church of Ralos?") and declarative statements/decisions ("I'd better be off.",
  "Alright, I'll get to it.").
- **Length:** Short — usually under 12 words; a handful run longer when the option itself
  conveys a plot decision ("You should return all these artefacts. It's the only right thing
  to do.").
- **Function split:** Options generally fall into three buckets — (1) lore/info questions, (2)
  task-progress acknowledgement ("Now's not the time for tea!"), (3) polite exits ("I'll make
  my own way there.", "Sorry, but I need to head off.").
- **Tone variety within one menu:** menus often offer one earnest option and one
  dry/skeptical option side by side, letting the player's personality bend slightly per
  choice while staying broadly polite.

## 8. Narration box conventions

- **Person/tense:** Second person, present tense, addressed directly to the player — "You
  show the crest to the knights.", "You search the chest and find some cultist robes."
- **Formula:** "You [verb] the [object]" or "You [verb] the [object] and [result]" is the
  dominant pattern for interaction feedback (search/find, show/react, pick/steal, use/unlock).
- **Third-person for NPC/world actions:** "Ennius kills Velam.", "Renu eagerly takes the feed
  from you.", "The gate is securely locked." — flat, factual, no editorializing.
- **Brevity:** Narration boxes are almost always a single short sentence; compound results use
  "and" rather than a second sentence.
- **No embellishment:** Narration stays neutral/matter-of-fact even during dramatic beats
  ("Alan falls to the ground, dying at the bottom of the stairs.") — the emotional weight is
  left to NPC dialogue that follows, not the narration itself.

## 9. Varlamore-specific flavour

- **Ralos:** invoked as the regional faith/blessing reference in formal or earnest speech —
  e.g. "...and with the guidance of Ralos..." (*The Final Dawn*), and church/sect references
  ("Could you tell me more about the Church of Ralos?", "the Sect of Ranul" — *Twilight's
  Promise*), signalling competing religious factions within the Kingdom.
- **The Kingdom / Civitas illa Fortis:** referred to as "the capital" and via city officials
  (Curator Herminius, Regulus Cento) who use Roman-flavoured formal names (Tullus, Cento,
  Vibia, Katlo) and a distinct greeting vocabulary — "Nilsal" (hello/welcome) and "iknami"
  (a respectful address to the player), both used consistently by Fortis-culture NPCs.
- **Guards:** procedural, rank-conscious, quick to cite orders/rules over judgment ("Well
  rules are rules, I'm afraid."), but immediately deferential to royal seals/crests.
- **Colosseum-adjacent officials (Highlord, Captain of the guard):** speak in short command
  sentences and formal grants of permission ("I grant you permission to search for these
  cultists." — Highlord Katlo).
- **Sea/harbour references:** shipping/trade merchants have a boastful commercial patter —
  "I run something of a shipping company. If you need it moving, Xocotla's Got Ya!" (*Death on
  the Isle*) — and sailors/traders speak evasively about cargo and passengers when questioned.
- **Locals vs. outsiders:** Kitchen/dwarf/tribal NPCs (Cam Torum dwarves, jaguar-worshipping
  Aldarin-adjacent farmers) default to suspicion of the player as "human"/outsider, softening
  only through earned trust; Fortis city-dwellers are more used to travellers and default to
  polished, transactional politeness.

## 10. Do / Don't rules

**Do:**
1. Keep NPC lines to one or two short sentences; save long speeches for named lore-heavy
   characters only.
2. Open refusals with a soft hedge word ("Sorry," "Well," "I'm afraid") before the reason.
3. Use ellipses sparingly to mark hesitation or a trailing thought, not for suspense padding.
4. Write narration boxes as flat, second-person "You [verb] the [noun]" statements with no
   editorializing.
5. Give officials/guards a rule they cite mechanically, even when it's clearly inconvenient.
6. Let one throwaway NPC per quest have a vain catchphrase or self-announced job title.
7. Use British spelling consistently (honour, colour, realise, defence, artefact, centre).
8. Reserve exclamation marks for genuine surprise, comic punchlines, or established
   in-world greetings — not general emphasis.
9. Keep player dialogue options short, mixing 1 info-seeking question with 1 decisive
   statement per menu.
10. Let status and props (a crest, a seal, a title) flip an NPC's tone from hostile/curt to
    deferential in a single line.

**Don't:**
1. Don't stack more than two sentences in a single dialogue box outside major lore dumps.
2. Don't have NPCs over-explain their own jokes — land the deadpan line and move on.
3. Don't give bare refusals with no justification clause; always attach a reason or redirect.
4. Don't let narration boxes carry emotional adjectives — leave feeling to the dialogue that
   follows.
5. Don't use American spellings (color/honor/realize/artifact) anywhere in-world.
6. Don't make every guard or official uniformly rude — vary curtness with occasional dry
   warmth so it doesn't read as one-note.
7. Don't give the player cruel or mocking lines toward NPCs; sarcasm should stay
   self-deprecating or mild.
8. Don't write option menus with more than 4-5 choices; trim to the essential branches.
9. Don't drop "human"/outsider dismissiveness onto every non-human NPC uniformly — reserve it
   for specific factions (dwarves, tribal groups) to keep it meaningful.
10. Don't overuse ellipses or exclamation marks in the same box — pick one device per line,
    not both.
