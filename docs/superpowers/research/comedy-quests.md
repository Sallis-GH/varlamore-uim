# OSRS Comedy-Quest Dialogue Analysis (2004-2007 Era)

Research slice: the comedy-heaviest quests of the mid-2000s OSRS era, gathered to give a plugin author a feel for authentic NPC voice. All quotes are verbatim, under 125 characters, and attributed to speaker and source quest. Sourced via OSRS Wiki quest transcripts.

## 2. Sentence and Line Shape

- **Line length is short by design.** The large majority of NPC lines sit in the 20-70 character band (roughly 4-15 words). Long expository lines (80-125 chars) exist but are outliers, usually lore-dumps or one "big reveal" line per NPC.
- **Extremes are used for effect, not information.** One-word or fragment lines ("Egad...", "Silence!!", "No!", "Charming") land jokes or punctuate tension. Nobody delivers two long sentences in a row without a short one breaking the rhythm.
- **Dialect is phonetic, not grammatical rewriting.** Ogres, gnomes-in-disguise, cockney ghosts and pirates all keep normal word order and vocabulary; only spelling and a few stock words change ("da," "dis," "yous," "ye," "arr," "moivellous"). This keeps lines readable at a glance while signalling class/species instantly.
- **Ellipses and interjections do a lot of comic work** ("...oh, what have I done...", "Nope, not yet", "Egad...") — they signal a beat/pause a human voice actor would take.
- **Repetition-with-escalation is a rhythm device**: Captain Donnie's "Hahahahahaha! Bwahahahaha!", Dr Fenkenstrain's "theyrecomingtogetme theyrecomingtogetme" — panic or menace rendered as unbroken repeated text rather than described.

## 3. Humour Catalogue (ranked by observed frequency)

### 1. Deadpan/dry understatement (most common device)
Delivering a wildly disproportionate situation in a flat, minimising register.
- "Well, that doesn't surprise me much." — Bill Teach, Cabin Fever
- "This is no time for humour." — Bailey, Sea Slug
- "We lost three diving parties that way!" — Murphy, Freeing Pirate Pete (said cheerfully)
- "I have my castle back, if not my soul." — Rologarth, Creature of Fenkenstrain
**Rule:** state a disturbing or absurd fact in the same neutral tone you'd use for the weather.

### 2. Bureaucratic/procedural absurdity
Officials treating danger, death, or the fantastical as paperwork.
- "No ID, no entry!" — KGP Agent, Cold War
- "Right answer. I have to watch for imposters." — Foreman, The Grand Tree
- "The translation must be perfect or the information's no use." — King Narnode, The Grand Tree
- "I'm sorry, I am not authorised to talk to you." — Waydar, Monkey Madness I
**Rule:** give the guard/official a rule, a form, or a clearance level, and have them cite it instead of reacting to the plot.

### 3. Refusal disguised as deflection (evasive non-answers)
NPC won't say no outright — they redirect, demand more proof, or claim ignorance.
- "Because you can't. So that's fine." — Vestri/Austri, Fishing Contest
- "Oh, nothing to do with me then, never seen it in my life before!" — Sithik Ints, Zogre Flesh Eaters
- "I don't know what to say then, there isn't enough to go on with the clues." — Zavistic Rarve, Zogre Flesh Eaters
- "I can't afford to give away freebies in this business!" — Roavar, Creature of Fenkenstrain
**Rule:** never let an NPC just say "no" — make them justify it with a flimsy technicality.

### 4. Vanity / self-importance
NPCs rating their own looks, cleverness, or importance unprompted.
- "Oh well, at least I'm cleverer, prettier and will have a better bed." — Ava, Animal Magnetism
- "This jail cell is larger than my room in the Grand Tree!" — Carado, Monkey Madness I
- "A pity; you'd look quite cute with rabbit ears." — Turael/Aya, Animal Magnetism
- "Well it's about time. Run along now, I need some Robin time." — Robin, Ghosts Ahoy
**Rule:** let a minor NPC casually imply they are the most important character in the room.

### 5. Literal-mindedness / pedantry
Answering a rhetorical or emotional line with dead-literal logic.
- "What am I, an encyclopedia?" — Cook, Freeing Pirate Pete
- "One? How do you suppose to use a single nail?" — 'Black-eye', Tower of Life
- "You flapped a lot? I didn't think we could fly like other birds." — Penguin, Cold War
- "Do I resemble an iron-bar merchant?" — Ava, Animal Magnetism
**Rule:** have the NPC seize on the literal wording of the player's request and question its logic instead of answering.

### 6. Laziness / can't-be-bothered
Refusal rooted in simple unwillingness to work.
- "Stop bothering me, can't you see I'm busy!" — 'Gummy', Tower of Life
- "Quit bugging me then! I got me some fish to catch!" — Joshua, Fishing Contest
- "Don't you think some of us have work to do?" — Zavistic Rarve, Zogre Flesh Eaters
- "Can't...hurrr...see why not. This is firsty work doh." — 'The Guns', Tower of Life
**Rule:** the NPC's real objection is effort, not danger or morality — say so plainly.

### 7. Cowardice played for comedy
NPCs openly admitting fear instead of performing bravery.
- "Look, I think I'll wait here for someone a little more... you know... heroic." — Pirate Pete, Rum Deal
- "Oh Guthix, who am I kidding? We're all gonna die!" — Brewer 1, Rum Deal
- "You're not a pirate yet by a long shot!" — Bill Teach, Cabin Fever
- "I'm too old for that lark now though..." — Grandpa Jack, Fishing Contest
**Rule:** let a supposedly tough archetype (pirate, brewer, sailor) confess fear in plain language, undercutting the stereotype.

### 8. Non-sequitur / tonal whiplash
A line answering a different register than the one it was asked in.
- "Fantastic weather we're having today!" — Effigy, Tower of Life (mid-crisis)
- "Egbert no bacon." — Necrovarus, Ghosts Ahoy
- "I go' me sum hot 'wares, mate. Wotcha need?" — Noodle, Cold War
- "Fancy a crumpet? Sorry, I mean... Arr! Run him through sez I!" — Pirate 6, Cabin Fever
**Rule:** occasionally let an NPC break character register for one line, then snap back — the seam is the joke.

### 9. Insults to the player / needling
NPCs mocking the player's competence or credibility directly.
- "You look strange to me!" — Monkey Child, Monkey Madness I
- "Naaah...you no real deal." — Nung, Freeing Pirate Pete
- "You don't look very dangerous." — Fenkenstrain's Monster, Creature of Fenkenstrain
- "You don't strike me as being... devout, you know?" — Davey, Rum Deal
**Rule:** have minor NPCs openly doubt the player's heroism/credentials rather than deferring to them.

### 10. Dark comedy / gallows humour
Treating death, corpses, or violence lightly.
- "The corpses thing was just a hobby." — Dr Fenkenstrain, Creature of Fenkenstrain
- "I'll have ye flogged, hung, slapped with a haddock and sent back out there!" — Captain Braindeath, Rum Deal
- "Don't worry yourself, I'm not worried about bein' dead." — Gardener Ghost, Creature of Fenkenstrain
- "Just eat a cauliflower or something!" — Player, The Great Brain Robbery
**Rule:** name a gruesome consequence casually, in a list, alongside mundane items (a haddock next to a hanging).

### 11. Running-gag catchphrase / verbal tic
A character repeats a signature phrase across the quest for recognisability.
- "Da big crunchy claws" — Mogre Guard/Nung, Freeing Pirate Pete (repeated motif)
- "Da sickies" — Grish, Zogre Flesh Eaters (repeated across the quest)
- "Stabbers" for arrows, "bright pretties" for gold, "flufsies" — Big Chompy Bird Hunting ogres
- "Arr" / "ye" / "sez I" scattered through every pirate's lines as a rhythmic tic rather than one-off
**Rule:** give a recurring NPC one invented noun/phrase for an ordinary object and reuse it verbatim every time they mention it.

### 12. Self-aware/fourth-wall-adjacent humour (rarest, used sparingly)
- "Gah! This time travel stuff makes my head hurt!" — Player narration, Freeing Pirate Pete
- "And you call yourself an adventurer!" — Larry, Cold War
**Rule:** reserve fourth-wall winks for one line per quest at most — overuse breaks the comedic economy.

## 4. Refusal and Deflection Patterns

Outright, unqualified "no" is rare. The standard pattern is a three-part shape:
1. **Acknowledge the ask lightly** ("Well...", "Now you ask...", "Yes, I would love...").
2. **Attach a condition, technicality, or excuse** (not enough evidence, wrong item, wrong rank, busy, "not authorised").
3. **Close with a dismissal or redirect** ("Come back when...", "You should see X instead", "Go away and make that magnet").

Examples of the full shape:
- "Well, actually yes I do mind...it's my place and I don't want strangers going through my things." — Sithik Ints, Zogre Flesh Eaters
- "I'm sorry comrade, you do not have the clearance to go through these doors." — KGP Agent, Cold War
- "You seem to have no space for it. Come back when you do." — Rufus, The Great Brain Robbery (paraphrase pattern)
- "You were sent to try my patience, weren't you? Go away and make that magnet." — Witch, Animal Magnetism

Authority figures refuse with **rules and clearance**; working-class/comic NPCs refuse with **excuses and mild threats**; villains refuse with **outright menace** ("Silence!! Or I will remove your head." — Necrovarus, Ghosts Ahoy).

## 5. How NPCs Address and Treat the Player, and How Authority Changes Tone

- **Low-status/comic NPCs** treat the player as an annoyance or an unproven amateur: "You no real deal," "You look strange to me," "You too weedy," "You don't look very dangerous." The player must prove competence repeatedly — nothing is taken on trust.
- **Mid-status working NPCs** (shipyard workers, brewers, cooks) treat the player as an interruption to their job: curt, task-focused, occasionally rude ("Get out of here before I give you a beating!").
- **Authority figures** (kings, captains, doctors, agents) address the player formally, often by title ("Traveller," "human," "comrade," "lad"), and pair courtesy with a hard boundary: "Please Traveller, leave before it's too late!" Their tone is graver and their sentences longer than the comic NPCs around them — authority is partly signalled by lower joke density and by using full titles instead of nicknames.
- **Villains** are the only ones who escalate to raw threat register without deflection: "I will incinerate the flesh from your bones!!!" Their humour, when present, is cold rather than warm (Necrovarus's flat "Egbert no bacon").
- The player is rarely flattered. Respect must be earned mid-quest; NPCs who initially insult the player (Bill Teach, Nung, monkeys) soften only after a demonstrated feat, and even then keep a needling edge.

## 6. Player-Line Conventions

- The player is allowed real sarcasm and bravado, but it is undercut immediately by an NPC or by circumstance — it never lands as pure cool.
  - "I can't say the same for your glider!" — Player, The Grand Tree
  - "Oh, this is nothing. All in a day's work for me." — Player, The Great Brain Robbery (delivered right before something goes wrong)
  - "I think I'll make sure the crate is shipped upside down, and not give you padding." — Player, The Great Brain Robbery
  - "Just eat a cauliflower or something!" — Player, The Great Brain Robbery
- Player boasts and threats use the same register as NPC dialogue (short, punchy, one beat) — the player is not written with noticeably more eloquence than the NPCs around them.
- Player lines also carry the "admit weakness" beat used elsewhere: "Gah! This time travel stuff makes my head hurt!" (internal narration, comic complaint rather than heroic resolve).
- Genuine warmth/sincerity from the player exists but is brief and unembellished ("I'd be happy to help!", "Yes, please.") — sincerity is stated plainly, never purple.

## 7. Option-Menu Conventions

- Menus mix **one earnest/helpful option**, **one sarcastic or joke option**, and **one refusal/delay option**, e.g. Cabin Fever's "Yes, I've always wanted to be a pirate!" vs. "No, if it's so dangerous your crew left, count me out." vs. "Can I just go grab something?"
- Refusal options are almost always reversible/soft: "Not right now, thanks," "Not just yet," "I don't think I'm ready to hand it over yet." True dead-end refusals are rare in menus (quests still need to progress).
- Some options are pure flavour with no mechanical stakes, existing only to let the player be silly: "Well I'll be a monkey's uncle!", "What's a nice girl like you doing in a place like this?"
- Menus frequently offer an information-seeking option phrased as a plain question ("What do you mean sickies?", "Why do you talk like a pirate?") alongside an action option ("I've come to join your crew!") — curiosity and commitment are kept as separate choices.
- Aggressive/threatening player options exist but are played for comic excess rather than real menace: "Tell me sonny, or I will hurt you." (Gertrude's Cat).

## 8. Narration Box Conventions

- Narration boxes are almost always **short, declarative, present/past tense, second person** ("You climb down the steps," "You pull the statue, revealing the hole underneath," "The grave is empty").
- They stay mechanically neutral even when the on-screen event is absurd or violent: "You're hit by bits of exploding toad!", "The rope falls off the mast! Clearly your knots are sub-sailor standard." — the second half of that line is where the dry joke lives, riding on top of an otherwise flat mechanical report.
- Longer narration is reserved for scene-setting or cutscene description ("A cutscene starts with the player pondering on where to get the cats...") and stays functional rather than literary.
- Humour in narration boxes is rare and always understatement-based, never a joke construction of its own — it's one extra clause appended to a factual sentence.

## 9. Sea, Ship, Dock, Pirate, Customs and Guard Flavour

- **Pirate dialect markers** are consistent across Cabin Fever, Rum Deal, and Freeing Pirate Pete: "arr," "ye," "te" (to), "fer" (for), "sez," "lad/landlubber," "Davy Jones locker," "hearty/m'hearty," dropped g's. These are sprinkled, not applied to every word — readability over authenticity.
- **Ship-captain voice** is loud, command-driven, short imperative sentences: "Give me strength...", "Less talk, lad, haven't ye got a hopper to be fillin'?", "Right me hearty. Let's get this boat fixed."
- **Customs/military officials** (Cold War's KGP Agent, Monkey Madness's shipyard/military NPCs) are clipped and rule-citing, almost the opposite register of pirates: "Let's see your ID and you can go through," "No ID, no entry!", "You should report immediately to the King!" This is a deliberate contrast — nautical/outlaw characters ramble and colour their speech; officials clip theirs to bureaucratic minimum.
- **Sailors/crew under threat** speak in stock aggressive one-liners with no individuality: "Kill the landlubber!", "Te Davy-Jones locker with ye!", "I'll have ye guts fer garters!" — these are disposable-mob lines, intentionally interchangeable.
- **Docks/harbour NPCs** (Murphy in Freeing Pirate Pete, shipyard workers in Monkey Madness) mix practical safety warnings with dry professional humour: "I can't take you diving if you are carrying more than 27kg," "the bowls I make tend to shatter under pressure into razor-sharp shards."
- **Ghost sailors** (Ghosts Ahoy's Pirate Captain, old man, innkeeper) retain nautical vocabulary but flatten it with melancholy or dry deadpan: "Sorry, but our pumps dried up over half a century ago," "Hang on, let me ask the Captain..."

## 10. Do / Don't Rules

**Do:**
1. Keep the large majority of lines under ~70 characters; save long lines for one lore-dump per NPC.
2. Give every refusal a reason, however flimsy, rather than a flat "no."
3. Let low-status/comic NPCs openly doubt or insult the player's competence.
4. Use one recurring invented noun/phrase per comic NPC and repeat it verbatim ("bright pretties," "da sickies").
5. Undercut player boasts immediately with an NPC's dry response or bad luck.
6. Make guards/officials clip their sentences and cite rules or clearance rather than reacting emotionally.
7. Let pirates/sailors/outlaws ramble in dialect while officials stay terse — use register contrast as flavour.
8. Attach one dry, incongruous clause to otherwise mechanical narration boxes, sparingly.
9. Escalate panic or menace via repetition of a phrase rather than new descriptive text.
10. Let minor mob/disposable NPCs (guards being fought, hostile crew) share one interchangeable stock line each — don't individuate throwaway characters.

**Don't:**
1. Don't give NPCs uniformly polished, grammatically "correct" speech — dialect markers should vary by class/species.
2. Don't let refusals be permanent or unkind without a joke or excuse attached.
3. Don't let the player out-eloquence the world — keep player lines the same short, punchy register as NPCs.
4. Don't stack two long expository sentences back to back; break them with a short line or fragment.
5. Don't use fourth-wall/self-aware jokes more than once per quest — they lose force with repetition.
6. Don't make authority figures funny in the same way as comic NPCs — their humour, if any, should be colder or drier, not silly.
7. Don't let villains deflect — they should refuse with direct threat, not excuses.
8. Don't overload narration boxes with jokes; they are reporting mechanics first, editorializing second.
9. Don't give every NPC a unique catchphrase — reserve it for a handful of recurring, named characters.
10. Don't make dialect an obstacle to comprehension — keep grammar/word order normal even when spelling is altered.
