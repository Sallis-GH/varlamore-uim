# Voice samples: Varlamore dock-adjacent character types

Source: OSRS Wiki `Transcript:*` pages, fetched live. Pages that 404'd (no dedicated
transcript exists for that exact name) are noted inline so writers know where the
wiki gives us nothing and invention is unconstrained. All quotes verbatim, under
125 characters, with NPC + page attribution.

---

## 1. Guard / Fortis soldier

Wiki has no `Guard (Civitas illa Fortis)` or `Knight of Varlamore` transcript page
(both 404). `Guard` (generic) is Falador party-room overhead spam, not usable.
`Knight of Varlamore` has no dialogue transcript — only combat/drop stats exist.
Closest usable substitute for register: `Border Guard (Varlamore)`.

- "Can I help you?" — Border Guard (Varlamore)
- "This is the only land border into the Kingdom of Varlamore." — Border Guard (Varlamore)
- "We're here to make sure no one enters without permission." — Border Guard (Varlamore)
- "Sorry, traveller, but you don't have permission to enter the Kingdom of Varlamore." — Border Guard (Varlamore)
- "However, this is an inhospitable route into the kingdom." — Border Guard (Varlamore)
- "I'd suggest you find an alternate way to travel." — Border Guard (Varlamore)
- "Fair enough." — Border Guard (Varlamore)

**What this role knows/talks about:**
- Border Guard's whole vocabulary is one topic: who may or may not cross a line, on whose authority.
- Cites the Queen's arrangements as the legal basis for letting outsiders in — law flows from the Crown, not from the guard's own judgment.
- Uses "kingdom" as the formal name for Varlamore, never "the region" or "the island."
- Never jokes, never asks about the player's business beyond permission status.
- Never mentions the Colosseum, Ralos, or local gossip — a Fortis harbour guard invented from this base should stay similarly narrow and procedural.

**Writer note:** because no Fortis city-guard transcript exists, a harbour guard's
voice must be built by analogy to Border Guard (procedural, permission-obsessed)
plus Citizen (Civitas illa Fortis) (for "Nilsal" greeting and clipped small talk).

## 2. Harbourmaster / port official

No such NPC exists in-game at all — searches for "Harbourmaster Varlamore" and
"Customs officer Varlamore" both returned zero wiki results; only a generic
Karamja/Musa Point `Customs officer` exists. Useful as a register model only,
not as Varlamore-flavoured material.

- "Can I help you?" — Customs officer (Musa Point)
- "You need to be searched before you can board." — Customs officer
- "Spot inspection. You don't mind do you?" — Customs officer
- "Well you've got some odd stuff, but it's all legal." — Customs officer
- "Now you need to pay a boarding charge of 30 coins." — Customs officer
- "Aha, trying to smuggle rum are we?" — Customs officer
- "This is not the Karamja rum we are looking for." — Customs officer
- "You're not getting on this ship then." — Customs officer
- "I'm not that sort of customs officer." — Customs officer
- "Hey, I know you, you work at the plantation." — Customs officer

**What this role knows/talks about:**
- Fees, cargo legality, and inspections — a transactional gatekeeper voice, not a chatty one.
- Recognises frequent travellers/workers by sight ("I know you, you work at the plantation") — locals get a discount, strangers get searched.
- Running joke register: repeats near-identical "smuggling rum" lines as a bit, not fresh material each time.
- Cites a specific law/authority for a ban ("Asgarnia has banned...") rather than personal opinion — officials speak for institutions.
- Never discusses the sea itself, weather, or anything outside cargo/fees/permission — a Varlamore equivalent should invent Kingdom-specific contraband (Colosseum betting chips? unlicensed relics?) rather than reuse rum.
- **Gap for writers:** since no in-game Varlamore harbourmaster exists, this is the role with the most creative latitude, anchored only by the Border Guard's legalistic register and the Customs officer's fee/inspection structure.

## 3. Fisher

- "Hello! Nice day for fishing!" — Fisher
- "Well given how good today's catch was, I'd say it's going pretty well!" — Fisher
- "Nilsal! It's going well, thanks." — Fisher
- "I'm fine, how are you?" — Fisher
- "Not too bad thanks." — Fisher

**What this role knows/talks about:**
- The catch of the day, and whether it was good or bad — the only real "job content" in the lines.
- Greets using the Varlamore "Nilsal" alongside plain "Hello," code-switching depending on who's asked.
- Never names a specific fish, buyer, or price — commentary stays generic ("today's catch"), no shop talk about markets.
- Never complains about weather, tides, or competitors — notably upbeat compared to workers/beggars.
- Never mentions the Kingdom, Ralos, the Colosseum, or politics — a fisher's world is the water, full stop.

## 4. Vineyard hand / vintner (Aldarin)

Sources: `Vineyard worker`, `Antonius` (Moonrise sommelier/owner), `Lucrezia`
(Moonrise brewer — cider/ale only, no wine lines), `Shopkeeper (Aldarin)`.

- "Lovely day for some wine!" — Vineyard worker
- "I wish these grapes could pick themselves." — Vineyard worker
- "The grapes are in good shape for this harvest." — Vineyard worker
- "I'm sorry, but the vineyards need tending to." — Vineyard worker
- "Have you tried the wine? You won't regret it if you do!" — Vineyard worker
- "Welcome to the Moonrise Brewery and Winery, the oldest and largest producer of alcohol in all of Varlamore." — Antonius
- "The Moonrise Winery was originally opened over 400 years ago by Bracus Ramio." — Antonius
- "Ludovic found himself in a tragic and unexpected wine-related incident before ever having children himself." — Antonius
- "Some of the most influential people all across Varlamore have a stake in the Moonrise Brewery and Winery." — Antonius
- "What can I do for you, iknami?" — Shopkeeper (Aldarin)

**What this role knows/talks about:**
- The physical labour of grape-tending: picking, harvest condition, wishing the work were easier.
- Product pride — pushes the wine itself as a point of local excellence, not just a job.
- Estate history and lineage (Bracus Ramio founding it, grandson Ludovic inventing sunbeam ale) — vineyard people carry generational company lore.
- Notes that the wealthy and "influential people all across Varlamore" quietly own stakes — a hint of old money behind the vines, delivered dryly.
- Never mentions the sea, the Colosseum, or religion — a purely agricultural/commercial register, and workers never grumble about pay or owners directly (that critique is left to the sommelier's dry aside about anonymous investors).
- Uses "iknami" as a term of address to the player (Aldarin shopkeeper) — a local honorific/term for outsider or customer, distinct from Fortis's "Nilsal."

## 5. Priest / pilgrim of Ralos (and Ranul)

Sources: `Nelta of Ralos`, `Nelta of Ranul`.

- "Nilsal, traveller." — Nelta of Ralos
- "I am a nelta in service to Ralos." — Nelta of Ralos
- "We are the faithful servants of Ralos." — Nelta of Ralos
- "The first and only time that Ralos appeared to us, he did so here." — Nelta of Ralos
- "May the sun light your way." — Nelta of Ralos
- "I am a nelta of the Sect of Ranul." — Nelta of Ranul
- "Our primary duty is to care for the dead and prepare them for their journey through the Gates of Mictl." — Nelta of Ranul
- "This is the Teomat. Both the Church of Ralos and the Sect of Ranul are based here." — Nelta of Ranul

**What this role knows/talks about:**
- Their own rank and order by name — "nelta" is the lowest clergy rank of Ralos or Ranul, and they self-identify with it plainly, not humbly.
- A specific, place-bound miracle claim (Ralos appearing "here," at a named site) — faith is anchored to geography, not abstract.
- Ranul's clergy specifically own death/funerary duty and can name the afterlife destination ("Gates of Mictl") — precise theology, not vague comfort.
- Blessing-style sign-offs tied to their god's domain (sun for Ralos) rather than generic well-wishes.
- Never discuss the Colosseum's blood-offering doctrine directly in these lines (that's attributed to the wiki's summary of the site, not nelta dialogue) and never mention money, trade, or politics — clergy voice stays inside faith and duty.

## 6. Mysterious old man / hermit

Sources: `Mysterious Old Man` (minigame host, not lore-Varlamore but the definitive
"strange old man" register), `Wise Old Man` (generic sage register), `Attala`
(Cam Torum dwarf elder — closest in-game Varlamore-adjacent "keeper of old
grudges/history" voice).

- "Hey, [player name], would you like to come and solve a maze for me?" — Mysterious Old Man
- "Ah, so you are there. I hoped you would talk to me, I get so lonely." — Mysterious Old Man
- "Here, take this. But tell no one I was here." — Mysterious Old Man
- "Sorry, I'm trying to speak to someone else, but he seems to be ignoring me." — Mysterious Old Man
- "I'm sorry, but you are no use to me." — Mysterious Old Man
- "Less of the 'old' man, if you please!" — Wise Old Man
- "I prefer to think of myself as a sage." — Wise Old Man
- "Naturally, most blame you humans." — Attala
- "The Varlamorians won't mention this, but we were the first to settle here." — Attala
- "We remember our history, and we know that those who don't are doomed to repeat it." — Attala
- "Who knows, maybe one or two dwarves here might even grow to tolerate you." — Attala

**What this role knows/talks about:**
- Loneliness and cryptic errands — the Mysterious Old Man's whole shtick is odd requests with unstated stakes, secrecy demanded afterward ("tell no one I was here").
- Correcting assumptions about age/status ("Less of the 'old' man") — pride hides under the frail exterior.
- Attala (the nearest real Varlamore "old keeper of grudges") holds deep institutional memory that contradicts the official Varlamorian narrative, and delivers it reluctantly, sarcastically.
- Long historical memory used as a rebuke ("doomed to repeat it") rather than nostalgia.
- Grudging, conditional warmth toward the player rather than open friendliness — approval must be earned, and even then it's hedged ("maybe one or two").

## 7. Beggar / vagrant

No dedicated `Beggar` OSRS transcript exists (search returns only incidental
mentions of "beggar" inside unrelated quest transcripts). Nearest in-game
analogue for tone is the Wealthy citizen's dismissal of beggars and the generic
`Drunken Man` (non-Varlamore, Falador) for a down-and-out register.

- "No! We don't care for begging in the city!" — Wealthy citizen
- "As you can tell, I'm doing quite well winning! Plenty of coin to pass around." — Wealthy citizen
- "Why are there two of you?" — Drunken Man (Falador, non-Varlamore, register only)

**What this role knows/talks about:**
- We only see this role from the outside (a wealthy citizen's contempt) — no first-person beggar voice exists in Varlamore content at all.
- The wealthy citizen's line confirms begging is treated as a civic nuisance/shame in Civitas illa Fortis, officially unwelcome.
- **Gap for writers:** a Varlamore beggar has zero verbatim source material; invent from the city's own class-conscious tone (wealthy citizens flaunt winnings, openly refuse beggars) rather than generic OSRS drunk/tramp tropes.
- If a Varlamore beggar is written, keep the surrounding world's coldness in mind — no wiki NPC shows sympathy toward begging.
- Avoid borrowing the Drunken Man's slapstick ("two of you") — it's Falador comic relief, tonally off for Varlamore's harder-edged class references.

## 8. Sailor / dockworker

Sources: `Sailor (Varlamore)`, `Trader Crewmember` (Karamja trade fleet, register
model), `Sailor` (generic Fremennik, register only). No dedicated "Dockworker"
page exists anywhere in the wiki (zero search results).

- "Okay, but it will be better once we're back on board." — Sailor (Varlamore)
- "Sorry, but I have lots of work to do before we head back out to sea." — Sailor (Varlamore)
- "I'm one of Trader Stan's crew." — Trader Crewmember
- "We are all part of one of the largest fleet of trading and sailing vessels to ever sail the seas." — Trader Crewmember
- "If you want to get to a port in a hurry, then you can charter one of our ships." — Trader Crewmember
- "The tortugans were reluctant at first, but we eventually convinced them to let us do business." — Trader Crewmember
- "It's a shame they won't let us actually go beyond the docks though." — Trader Crewmember
- "I'm afraid we can't take you anywhere if you're trying to smuggle that rum." — Trader Crewmember
- "Sorry, but we aren't a laundry ship." — Trader Crewmember
- "Don't talk to me outerlander. I need to fix this longboat." — Sailor (Fremennik, register only)

**What this role knows/talks about:**
- Being mid-shift, always about to go back to sea or too busy for chat right now — sailors are perpetually between duties, not idle.
- Crew identity tied to a named captain/company ("Trader Stan's crew") and a list of real ports — sailors name-drop geography and employers, unlike fishers.
- Restrictions imposed on them by locals ("won't let us go beyond the docks") — friction with the place they're docked at is a running theme.
- Deadpan refusals of smuggling/illicit requests played for a small joke ("we aren't a laundry ship").
- Never discuss religion, politics, or the Kingdom's internal affairs — their world is cargo, ports, and getting back underway.

- Never mentions cost, itinerary logistics, or complaints — an idealised, uncomplicated visitor voice (contrast with a jaded local).

## 11. Merchant (Fur / Spice / Gem / Silk, Civitas illa Fortis bazaar)

Sources: `Fur Merchant`, `Spice Merchant`. `Gem Merchant` and `Silk Merchant`
transcript pages 404 (not yet documented on wiki) — treat as same template by
analogy given both confirmed merchants share near-identical lines.

- "Hello there. Want to trade some furs?" — Fur Merchant
- "I have a selection of both rare and common furs from all across the kingdom." — Fur Merchant
- "If you happen to have any furs you've brought from the east, I'd be pretty keen to buy some." — Fur Merchant
- "So, want to trade?" — Fur Merchant
- "Tetamo! If you change your mind, you know where to find me." — Fur Merchant
- "You're the one who stole from me! Guards! Guards!" — Fur Merchant
- "A little bit of spice makes everything taste nice! Come and get it here!" — Spice Merchant
- "Your loss." — Spice Merchant
- "You're the one who stole from me! Guards! Guards!" — Spice Merchant

**What this role knows/talks about:**
- Their specific stock and its origin ("all across the kingdom," goods "from the east") — merchants are proud of sourcing/variety, not just price.
- A rhyming sales pitch as a verbal tic (Spice Merchant's "makes everything taste nice") — merchants get one catchy hook line each, unlike other roles.
- A curt, slightly wounded reaction to a declined sale ("Your loss.") — brush-offs are short and a bit petty, not polite.
- Immediately escalate to calling guards if the player has stolen from them — merchants treat theft as a hard breach, not banter.
- Use "Tetamo" as a farewell/thanks when a customer walks away — a second confirmed Varlamore-specific term of address alongside "Nilsal" and "iknami."

## 12. Guild / official-adjacent civic voice (bonus: Hunter Guild, border officials)

Useful for harbourmaster-analogue writers since it's the most "official Varlamore
civil servant" voice on record.

- "Nilsal, hunter." — Guildmaster Apatura
- "Well, I'm Apatura. Let me welcome you to our small corner of Varlamore." — Guildmaster Apatura
- "We're mostly here to keep an eye on the local animal populations, and provide animal parts to the city." — Guildmaster Apatura
- "A hunter's work is dangerous. We tend to go through members faster than other guilds." — Guildmaster Apatura
- "I'm usually too busy with politics and paperwork to go out hunting these days." — Guildmaster Apatura
- "Your commitment to helping our guild is commendable, iknami." — Guildmaster Apatura
- "Nilsal, friend. I don't believe you're ready to go down into the Burrow." — Guildmaster Apatura

**What this role knows/talks about:**
- Frames their post as service to "the city" (supplying animal parts) — civic duty phrased as a supply relationship to Fortis/Aldarin.
- Openly admits the job has moved from fieldwork to "politics and paperwork" — a specific, self-deprecating admission of bureaucratic drift a harbourmaster could echo.
- Tracks the player's standing/reputation explicitly and rewards it verbally before mechanically ("commendable").
- Warns the player off dangerous areas by asserting authority over readiness, not just posting a rule.
- Mixes both "Nilsal" and "iknami" address terms interchangeably — confirms these two are not exclusive to one district, both circulate kingdom-wide.

## Register notes

- **Line length:** most ordinary-citizen and worker lines run 4-14 words (e.g.
  "Not too bad thanks." / "Sorry, but I'm busy right now."). Merchant pitch
  lines and lore-dispensing NPCs (Antonius, Attala, Guildmaster Apatura) run
  longer, 15-30 words, when explaining history or duty — length scales with
  how much institutional knowledge the speaker is expected to hold.
- **Contractions/fragments:** contractions are constant and unmarked ("I'm,"
  "don't," "won't," "I'd," "we're") — nobody in Varlamore speaks formally by
  default. Fragments appear as one-line brush-offs: "Fair enough." "Your loss."
  "Take a look." "Correct!" — officials and shopkeepers favor these clipped
  closers over full sentences.
- **Jokes land in one beat:** humour is almost always a single short line with
  no build-up or callback — "We aren't a laundry ship." "This is not the
  Karamja rum we are looking for." "Why are there two of you?" There is no
  multi-line comic escalation in any sampled transcript; a joke is delivered
  and the NPC moves on.
- **Punctuation habits:** exclamation points mark greetings, sales pitches, and
  alarm ("Nilsal!" "Guards! Guards!" "A little bit of spice makes everything
  taste nice!") but are absent from complaints, refusals, and lore exposition,
  which favour periods even when the content is dramatic ("Ludovic found
  himself in a tragic and unexpected wine-related incident."). Questions are
  rare outside of greetings ("How are you liking the city?") and shop prompts.
- **Address terms as flourish:** "Nilsal," "iknami," and "Tetamo" function like
  seasoning dropped into otherwise plain English sentences rather than replacing
  whole phrases — NPCs default to English syntax and only sprinkle in one
  Varlamorian word per line, never a full sentence of invented language.
